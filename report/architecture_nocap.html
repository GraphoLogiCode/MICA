Figure 1. (a) Overview of MICA, an intention-aware collaborative Minecraft agent. Each game tick, an observation packet is split into two parallel perception streams — 2D behaviour (frozen VPT and MineCLIP backbones, symbolic state, and a rule-based macro-action) and 3D spatial (event-sourced voxel replay, per-goal template features, and a frozen Uni3D embedding). A trained adapter Aφ fuses the role-typed evidence and two likelihood heads produce a goal- and mode-conditioned action likelihood P(ak | ek, g, z). A recursive belief tracker (highlighted; our contribution) maintains an explicit distribution bk(g, z) over five goals × two reasoning modes through a predict→correct rhythm, and is the only module swapped across the four experiment arms. Its control context ck conditions a multi-token action decoder with a staircase commit gate and a safety-gate FSM that emits the final assistance action. Colour encodes role — frozen pretrained (blue), trained (orange), rule-based / symbolic and data contracts (grey); one-way constraints ①–③ mark where feedback is deliberately absent (the idle cue is additionally a training target only — never a head feature — after a leak audit). Placement is licensed by confidence or by the human’s explicit chat consent (one block per “yes”); the decoder’s next-token stage is pretrained label-free on real sessions.
